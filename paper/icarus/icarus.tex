\title{Rotation periods and spin senses of asteroids from Gaia DR3 sparse photometry: calibrated reliability, null tests and disputed LCDB periods}

\author{Oleg Tropynin}
\ead{tropininoleg@gmail.com}
\address{Independent researcher, Karaganda, Kazakhstan}

\begin{abstract}
I derive rotation periods and spin senses for numbered asteroids with at least 25 Gaia DR3 transits, giving
41{,}842 solutions. The model fits a separate brightness level for each observing season, a linear phase
term and a second-order Fourier series. The rotational phase is corrected for the phase-angle bisector,
with both senses of rotation tried. Known LCDB periods are split once into a tuning half and a test half:
the period-doubling rule and a logistic model of the probability of a correct period are fitted on the
first, and the confidence threshold comes from null tests that preserve the Gaia sampling but remove
rotation. 6{,}238 periods reach the confident tier. On the test half, 89.3\% of them are correct within 3\%,
and 95.4\% where the reference quality is $U\ge3-$; injected synthetic lightcurves are recovered with
96--99\% precision. The spin sense agrees with published poles for 98.2\% of asteroids in an analysis of the
same data and for 94.4\% of independent DAMIT models. The senses reproduce the known Yarkovsky segregation in
collisional families: 74.8\% of 2{,}345 members lie on the predicted side, compared with $52.4\pm0.9\%$
when senses are permuted within families, and the fraction rises to 82\% for the most drifted members.
I identify 108 LCDB periods disputed by independent survey data or by agreement among Gaia DR3 analyses.
Nine more were already revised in the 2023 LCDB to the values found here. I list 861 candidate new periods,
with at most 4.5\% expected noise.
\end{abstract}
