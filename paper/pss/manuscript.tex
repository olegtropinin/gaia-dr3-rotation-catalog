\title{Rotation periods and spin senses of asteroids from Gaia DR3 sparse photometry: calibrated reliability, null tests and disputed LCDB periods}

\author{Oleg Tropynin}
\ead{tropininoleg@gmail.com}
\address{Independent researcher, Karaganda, Kazakhstan}

\begin{abstract}
I derive rotation periods and spin senses for numbered asteroids with at least 25 Gaia DR3 transits,
giving 41{,}842 solutions. The model fits one brightness level per observing season, a linear phase term
and a second-order Fourier series, with the rotational phase corrected for the phase-angle bisector under
both senses of rotation. Known LCDB periods are split into a tuning half and a test half. A logistic
model of the probability that a period is correct is fitted on the first, and the confidence threshold
comes from null tests that keep the Gaia sampling but remove rotation. 6{,}238 periods reach the
confident tier. On the test half 89.3\% of them are correct within 3\%, and 95.4\% where the reference
quality is $U\ge3-$; injected lightcurves are recovered with 96--99\% precision. Against independent ATLAS
data, 92.5\% of the periods and 96.4\% of the spin senses agree. 2{,}270 spin senses are for asteroids
without a published pole. The senses reproduce the Yarkovsky segregation in collisional families: 74.8\% of
2{,}345 members lie on the predicted side, against $52.4\pm0.9\%$ when senses are permuted within families.
I identify 108 LCDB periods contradicted by independent survey data (39) or disputed by agreement among
Gaia DR3 analyses (69). Finally, 790 confident periods appear in none of the sources searched, including
SsODNet; at most 4.5\% of them are noise, and about a quarter may be harmonics or aliases.
\end{abstract}
